====================================================================
THE PRINCIPLE — two layers, two sources
====================================================================
  JD    -> ROLE FLOOR (pass/fail)   "Is this the role Kargo needs?"
  HIRES -> RANKING WEIGHTS          "Of those, who looks like Kargo's best people?"

A criterion carries weight ONLY if the hire data separates Exceeds from Meets/Below.
Every criterion is labelled with its source. Where a criterion also appears in the JD, that is
stated, and it is kept only because the hires confirm it.

====================================================================
SCORING
====================================================================
- Each criterion scored 1–5 with anchors for every level.
- Weighted score = sum( ((score - 1) / 4) * weight )  -> 0–100 (all 1s = 0, all 5s = 100).
- Every candidate is scored against BOTH rubrics; flag if the other role fits 10+ points better.
- Every score above 2 needs a quote copied word-for-word from the CV. Code checks the quote against
  the CV text; if it is not found, the score is capped at 2 and flagged.
- Code-enforced rules (not left to the model):
    * SPM integration depth is capped at 3 unless ops-native >= 3 (the Preetham lesson).
    * A feature kill counts under "Ships and kills" only, never under "Owns a loss".

====================================================================
EVIDENCE RULES (from the evidence-first hiring philosophy)
====================================================================
The system never decides whether a candidate is "good". For every candidate it answers:
  1. What has this person actually demonstrated?   (quoted evidence per criterion)
  2. At what scope?                                  (demonstrated scope vs title; activity -> output ->
                                                      outcome -> business impact)
  3. What is not demonstrated?                       (evidence gaps)
  4. What must a human verify?                       (interview cards: question, strong answer, red flag)
- Evidence -> interpretation -> confidence are kept separate. Each score carries a confidence
  (high / medium / low); a missing or unverifiable quote is always low confidence.
- Absence of evidence is not absence of ability: "X is not demonstrated on the CV", never
  "the candidate lacks X". Decline emails never tell a candidate what they lack.
- Keywords, titles and verbs like "led" are not evidence. Problem -> decision -> action -> result is.
- Deliberately NOT adopted from the general philosophy, and why:
    * An 18-dimension generic capability model — the hire data shows generic PM dimensions
      (stakeholder management, quantified outcomes, business metrics) do not separate Kargo's
      best hires from average ones; weighting them would re-create JD matching.
    * No ranking at all — the brief asks for a ranked shortlist. The ranking is kept, but it is
      explained, carries confidence, and only produces drafts.
    * Candidate-facing view and startup/MNC toggle — one user (Arjun), one context (Series A).

====================================================================
ROLE FLOOR (pass/fail, not weighted) — this is where the JD lives
====================================================================
PM floor:  1+ yr in a product role owning a product/problem area AND one shipped outcome with a number.
           Students, freshers, pure marketers, project/delivery managers, pure engineers/analysts fail.
SPM floor: 4+ yrs of product ownership (not delivery) AND consequential product calls with no layer of
           senior PMs above (sole/most-senior/founding PM, or owned a platform area end-to-end).
Below the floor: still scored, still gets a draft, ranked below everyone who passes, flagged.
Strong-operator flag: below the floor but ops-native >= 4 -> "consider for ops / CS / solutions role"
before declining. Kargo's best hires look like this, and Kargo is hiring 30 more people by December.

====================================================================
INTERVIEW PROBES (what a CV cannot show — asked of every shortlisted candidate)
====================================================================
- "Tell me about something you built that nobody asked for. Who used it?"
- "Tell me about a call you made with nobody above you to check it. What happened?"
- "What's something you killed or lost, and what did you change afterwards?"
Arjun logs a 1–5 per probe, so CV scores can be checked against interview reality.

====================================================================
RECOMMENDATION (the system recommends — Arjun decides)
====================================================================
Top 5 per role (floor-passers first, then by score) -> draft interview invite.
Everyone else -> draft (NOT send) a respectful decline.
Arjun can override either way and confirms every single send. Nothing is auto-rejected or auto-sent.

====================================================================
VALIDATION (before scoring the 60 applicants)
====================================================================
The rubric is run on the 8 hire CVs first. Pass condition: Exceeds hires outscore Meets/Below hires,
and Lavanya is the top PM. Results are in validation-results.txt.
Second check after the 60 are scored: if the top 5 is almost entirely synthetic pm_/spm_ CVs, treat

====================================================================
PRIVACY
====================================================================
Scoring runs on CV content only. Name, email, phone and profile links are stripped in code before any
AI call; identity stays in the database and is re-attached only for display and at send time.
(College and company names are NOT stripped — pedigree is neutralised by instruction, not redaction.)
